Problem

For my introduction video I wanted to speak freely to the camera without losing a sentence. I needed a teleprompter that runs on a phone right next to the lens – with no installation, no account and without my text being uploaded anywhere.

Solution

Linelit is a web app that does exactly that and nothing else. The name says what it is about: one line is lit, the rest steps back. Open it for the first time and you see a sample text that explains the controls along the way, and one large start button.

  • Two ways to read: step through paragraph by paragraph, or let the text scroll by itself. The current line stays bright, the rest steps back.
  • Adjust while speaking: hold the left half of the screen to slow down, the right half to catch up – the pace you set stays untouched.
  • Target length instead of pace: enter 1:30 and the pace follows.
  • The reading marker can be moved, so your eyes stay where the camera is.
  • The clock shows elapsed or remaining time; the last ten seconds in amber.
  • Your own named texts, text files loaded by drag and drop and downloaded again as a file.
  • Optionally your own camera as the background: see yourself while reading and record with sound. The recording stays on the device and is only saved if you save it yourself.
  • Interface in German and English, usable by touch, mouse and keyboard.

Privacy

The app needs no account, no database and no tracking. Texts and settings live only in the browser on your own device – and only after you have saved or changed something yourself. There are no cookies.

The camera starts only when you tap it. Picture and sound stay in the device's memory and go to no server.

The page loads nothing from third-party servers: the fonts are bundled, and a Content Security Policy allows only its own origin. Everything on the device can be deleted again at any time.

Engineering

Deliberately without a framework and without a build step: HTML, CSS and JavaScript in ES modules, split by responsibility – state, stage, playback, text list, gestures and keys. Playback uses the time that actually passed in each frame, so the pace is the same on every device.

  • End-to-end tests with Playwright check, among other things, that no request goes to a third-party server and that nothing is stored before the first action of your own.
  • Widths from 340 to 1280 pixels are tested; on a phone the control bar stays on a single row.
  • Consistent formatting with Prettier, static analysis with SonarQube Cloud on every pull request.

Status

Linelit is live at linelit.frankdavidlee.com – a static site on Cloudflare Pages, with no server of its own. Every change on the main branch goes live automatically after the checks. The product tour shows the real app, recorded frame by frame.
